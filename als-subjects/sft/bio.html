Biology Notes

Living organisms, cellular organisation, microorganisms, plant structures, forests, tissue culture and economically important animals.

Syllabus-aligned study material based on the uploaded NIE Grade 12–13 Science for Technology syllabus. Sinhala explanations and further diagrams, examples and activities are being expanded chapter by chapter.
TOPIC 01

4.1 Cellular organisation

Cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Compare prokaryotic cells (no membrane-bound nucleus) with eukaryotic cells (membrane-bound nucleus and organelles). Study bacterial, fungal, plant and animal cell organisation; identify organelles and explain their functions.

Key notes

Cell membrane – selective boundary; cytoplasm – site of many reactions; nucleus – genetic control; mitochondria – aerobic respiration; ribosomes – protein synthesis. Plant cells additionally have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues and a large central vacuole.

TOPIC 02

4.2 Microorganisms

Microorganisms occur widely in soil, water, air and living bodies. Their small size, high metabolic activity and rapid reproduction allow adaptation to varied environments.

Key notes

Respiratory groups: aerobic, facultative anaerobic, obligate anaerobic and microaerophile. Nutritional modes include photoautotrophic, photoheterotrophic and chemoheterotrophic. Do not confuse oxygen requirement with nutritional source.

TOPIC 03

4.3 Technological applications

Microorganisms are used in food production and environmental technologies.

Key notes

Examples: yoghurt and curd production, alcohol fermentation, bakery, flavour production, vinegar, amino-acid production, composting, bioremediation and biogas. Notes should explain organism/process/product and the practical value.

TOPIC 04

4.4 Plant leaves, stems and roots

Compare monocotyledonous and dicotyledonous leaves and stems. Photosynthesis makes leaves major sites of carbohydrate production. Study dicot secondary growth and anomalous stem growth in the Palmae/Arecaceae group as specified in the syllabus.

Key notes

Economic value: leaves (food, fibre, medicine); stems (timber, construction, storage); roots (food, anchorage, storage and industrial uses). Hardwoods and softwoods are botanical/wood-property groupings, not simply a hardness test.

TOPIC 05

4.5 Forests and conservation

Distinguish primary and secondary forests. Study tropical wet evergreen, tropical dry mixed evergreen, tropical montane and coniferous forests, plus timber forests.

Key notes

Conservation strategies include sustainable use, protected reserves and afforestation. Link forest structure to climate, biodiversity, water regulation and economic value.

TOPIC 06

4.6 Plant tissue culture

Tissue culture is the controlled growth of plant cells/tissues on a sterile nutrient medium. The syllabus identifies apical meristem, immature leaf parts, buds, immature stems and stamens as possible explants.

Key notes

Core sequence: select explant → surface sterilise → prepare sterile nutrient medium → inoculate aseptically → induce callus/shoot/root development → acclimatise plantlets. Applications include rapid multiplication and production of disease-free planting material.

TOPIC 07

4.7 Economically important animals

Distinguish invertebrates from vertebrates. The syllabus highlights Arthropoda, prawn and honey bee, and chordate groups including bony fish, cartilaginous fish, birds and mammals.

Key notes

Compare body organisation and economic roles: food, pollination, fisheries, products and livelihoods. Use local examples where possible.
