Biochemistry Notes

Organic compounds and functional groups, carbohydrates, amino acids, proteins and lipids in biological systems and industry.

Syllabus-aligned study material based on the uploaded NIE Grade 12–13 Science for Technology syllabus. Sinhala explanations and further diagrams, examples and activities are being expanded chapter by chapter.
TOPIC 01

Organic compounds and functional groups

Biochemistry studies chemical substances and processes in living systems. Relate carbon compounds to biomolecules and identify functional groups in biochemical molecules.

Key notes

Key groups listed in the syllabus: hydroxyl (–OH), amino (–NH₂), carboxyl (–COOH), aldehyde (–CHO) and ketone (>C=O / –COR notation). Study polarity, electronegativity, hydrogen bonding, water solubility and acidity/basicity.

TOPIC 02

Carbohydrates

Classify carbohydrates as monosaccharides, disaccharides and polysaccharides. Study component elements, characteristic groups, biological importance and industrial applications.

Key notes

Glycosidic bonds join sugar units. Benedict's/Fehling's solutions test reducing sugars; iodine solution is used to identify starch. Record observation and inference separately.

TOPIC 03

Amino acids and proteins

Amino acids contain amino and carboxyl groups. They join through peptide bonds to form peptides and proteins. Study the ten essential amino acids named in the syllabus and protein importance.

Key notes

Primary structure is the amino-acid sequence; secondary structure includes regular folding patterns. Ninhydrin identifies free amino acids; Biuret reagent indicates proteins.

TOPIC 04

Lipids

Study the components, structure, occurrence, fats and oils, major groups and bonds. Relate structure to biological functions.

Key notes

Lipids are important in energy storage, membranes and insulation. Learn simple identification tests and connect observations with the presence of lipid.

TOPIC 05

Practical identification and applications

Use the syllabus tests as evidence-based identification rather than memorising colour changes alone.

Key notes

For each test record: sample, reagent, method, positive observation, inference and limitation. Follow laboratory safety instructions; never taste unknown samples.
