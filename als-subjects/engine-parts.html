Engine Systems
Parts & Operation

A visual study note covering three important internal-combustion engine types: four-stroke petrol, four-stroke diesel and two-stroke petrol engines. Identify their parts, compare their operation and learn the key differences.

Study note scope: The first two sections explain four-stroke petrol and diesel engines; the third explains a conventional crankcase-scavenged two-stroke petrol engine. Two-stroke diesel engines also exist, but their construction and scavenging systems differ. Diagrams are simplified learning illustrations, not repair instructions.

1. Engine cross-section — identify the parts

Start with the labelled cutaway. The top section controls the gases entering and leaving; the piston and connecting rod transfer force to the crankshaft below.

2. Main engine parts and their functions

1. Cylinder Block

The main body of the engine. It contains the cylinder bore(s) and supports many internal components.

එන්ජිමේ ප්‍රධාන බඳ කොටසයි. සිලින්ඩර සහ අනෙකුත් කොටස් සවිවන්නේ මෙයටයි.

2. Cylinder Head

Closes the top of the cylinder and commonly houses the valves, spark plug and combustion-chamber space.

සිලින්ඩරයේ ඉහළ කොටස වසා, වෑල්ව් සහ ස්පාර්ක් ප්ලග් වැනි කොටස් රඳවා ගනී.

3. Piston

Moves up and down inside the cylinder. Combustion pressure pushes it down during the power stroke.

සිලින්ඩරය තුළ ඉහළට හා පහළට ගමන් කරයි. දහනයෙන් ඇතිවන පීඩනයෙන් පහළට තල්ලු වේ.

4. Piston Rings

Seal the gap between piston and cylinder wall, help maintain compression and control lubricating oil.

පිස්ටනය හා සිලින්ඩර බිත්තිය අතර මුද්‍රාවක් ඇති කර සම්පීඩනය රඳවා තෙල් පාලනය කරයි.

5. Connecting Rod

Connects the piston to the crankshaft and transfers the piston force to the rotating shaft.

පිස්ටනය ක්‍රෑන්ක්ශාෆ්ට් එකට සම්බන්ධ කර බලය සම්ප්‍රේෂණය කරයි.

6. Crankshaft

Converts the piston's up-and-down (reciprocating) motion into rotary motion.

පිස්ටනයේ ඉහළ-පහළ චලිතය භ්‍රමණ චලිතයක් බවට පත් කරයි.

7. Inlet Valve

Opens at the correct time to let the fresh air–fuel charge enter the cylinder in a conventional port-injected/carburetted petrol engine.

අදාළ අවස්ථාවේ විවෘත වී වායු-ඉන්ධන මිශ්‍රණය සිලින්ඩරයට ඇතුළු වීමට ඉඩ දෙයි.

8. Exhaust Valve

Opens to allow burnt gases to leave the cylinder during the exhaust stroke.

පිටාර පහරේදී දහනය වූ වායු පිටතට යැවීමට විවෘත වේ.

9. Camshaft

Uses cam lobes to open the valves at the correct time. In a typical four-stroke engine it rotates once for every two crankshaft revolutions.

නිවැරදි වේලාවට වෑල්ව් විවෘත කරයි. සාමාන්‍ය four-stroke සැලසුමක ක්‍රෑන්ක්ශාෆ්ට් වට දෙකකට කැම්ශාෆ්ට් වටයක් භ්‍රමණය වේ.

10. Spark Plug

Produces an electric spark to ignite the compressed air–fuel mixture in a petrol engine.

පෙට්‍රල් එන්ජිමේ සම්පීඩිත වායු-ඉන්ධන මිශ්‍රණය දල්වීමට විදුලි පුළිඟුවක් නිපදවයි.

11. Combustion Chamber

The space above the piston where the air–fuel mixture is compressed and burns.

පිස්ටනයට ඉහළින් ඇති, මිශ්‍රණය සම්පීඩනය වී දහනය වන අවකාශයයි.

12. Crankcase & Oil Sump

The crankcase encloses/supports the crankshaft area. The sump stores engine lubricating oil in many wet-sump designs.

ක්‍රෑන්ක්ශාෆ්ට් කොටස ආවරණය කරයි. සාමාන්‍ය wet-sump පද්ධතියේ ලිහිසි තෙල් ගබඩා වන්නේ oil sump තුළයි.

3. How the four-stroke cycle works

Watch the animation, then read the four stages below. One complete cycle takes two crankshaft revolutions (720°).

STROKE 01

Intake / Suction

ආදාන පහර

The piston moves down. The inlet valve opens and the fresh charge enters the cylinder; the exhaust valve remains closed.

STROKE 02

Compression

සම්පීඩන පහර

The piston moves up with both valves closed, compressing the air–fuel mixture. Near the end, the spark plug fires.

STROKE 03

Power / Expansion

බල පහර

Combustion raises pressure and pushes the piston down. The connecting rod turns the crankshaft; both valves remain closed.

STROKE 04

Exhaust

පිටාර පහර

The piston moves up and pushes burnt gases out through the open exhaust valve; the inlet valve remains closed.

5. Four-stroke diesel engine — parts and operation

A diesel engine is a compression-ignition (CI) engine. Unlike a conventional petrol engine, it draws air alone into the cylinder, compresses it until it becomes very hot, and then injects diesel fuel. The fuel ignites because of the hot compressed air; a spark plug is not used for normal ignition.

Important diesel-specific parts

Fuel Injector

Sprays finely atomised diesel fuel into the combustion chamber at the required time and pressure.

නිවැරදි වේලාවට ඩීසල් ඉන්ධන කුඩා බිඳිති ලෙස දහන කුටියට විදියි.

Fuel Injection Pump / Common Rail

Supplies and controls high-pressure fuel for the injectors. The exact arrangement depends on the engine design.

ඉන්ජෙක්ටර් වෙත අවශ්‍ය පීඩනයෙන් ඉන්ධන සැපයීම හා පාලනය සිදු කරයි.

Glow Plug (where fitted)

Helps cold starting by heating the combustion chamber area. It does not ignite fuel during normal running.

සීතල අවස්ථාවේ එන්ජිම පණගැන්වීමට උපකාරී වේ. සාමාන්‍ය ධාවනයේදී ස්පාර්ක් ප්ලග් එකක් ලෙස ක්‍රියා නොකරයි.

High-pressure fuel line / Rail

Carries pressurised fuel from the pump or rail to the injector in applicable systems.

අධි පීඩන ඉන්ධන ඉන්ජෙක්ටරය වෙත ගෙන යයි.

STROKE 01

Intake / Suction

වායු ආදාන පහර

The piston moves down and the inlet valve opens. Air alone enters the cylinder.

STROKE 02

Compression

වායු සම්පීඩන පහර

Both valves close. The piston moves up and compresses the air, raising its temperature. Diesel is injected near the end of compression.

STROKE 03

Power

බල පහර

The injected fuel ignites in the hot compressed air. Expanding gases push the piston down and turn the crankshaft.

STROKE 04

Exhaust

පිටාර පහර

The exhaust valve opens and the piston moves up, pushing burnt gases out.

Remember: A diesel engine uses compression ignition. The injector supplies fuel; the high temperature of compressed air initiates combustion. A glow plug, if fitted, is mainly a cold-start aid.

6. Two-stroke petrol engine — parts and operation

A conventional two-stroke petrol engine completes one operating cycle in two piston strokes (one crankshaft revolution). It combines the gas-exchange processes with the compression and power movements. Many small engines use ports in the cylinder wall instead of poppet inlet and exhaust valves.

Main parts of a crankcase-scavenged two-stroke petrol engine

Cylinder & Cylinder Head

Form the working cylinder and combustion chamber.

සිලින්ඩරය සහ දහන කුටිය සාදයි.

Piston & Piston Rings

Move inside the cylinder, compress the charge and receive the force of combustion.

මිශ්‍රණය සම්පීඩනය කර දහන බලය ලබා ගනී.

Crankcase

In many small two-stroke petrol engines, the crankcase helps pre-compress the fresh air–fuel mixture.

කුඩා two-stroke පෙට්‍රල් එන්ජින්වල නව මිශ්‍රණය පෙර සම්පීඩනය කිරීමට දායක වේ.

Inlet Port

Allows fresh air–fuel mixture to enter the crankcase in the common crankcase-scavenged design.

නව වායු-ඉන්ධන මිශ්‍රණය crankcase එකට ඇතුළු කරයි.

Transfer Port

Passage that carries the fresh charge from the crankcase into the cylinder.

crankcase එකේ ඇති මිශ්‍රණය සිලින්ඩරයට ගෙන යයි.

Exhaust Port

Allows burnt gases to leave the cylinder as the piston uncovers the port.

පිස්ටනයෙන් විවෘත වන විට දහන වායු පිටතට යවයි.

Spark Plug

Ignites the compressed petrol–air mixture near the end of compression.

සම්පීඩිත පෙට්‍රල්-වායු මිශ්‍රණය දල්වයි.

Carburettor / Fuel System

In a carburetted example, mixes petrol and air before the charge enters the engine. Modern designs may use other fuel systems.

සාම්ප්‍රදායික carburettor පද්ධතියේදී පෙට්‍රල් හා වාතය මිශ්‍ර කරයි.

STROKE 01 · UPWARD

Compression + Crankcase Intake

සම්පීඩනය සහ මිශ්‍රණය ඇතුළුවීම

The piston moves upward and compresses the charge in the cylinder. At the same time, the increasing crankcase volume draws a fresh charge into the crankcase through the inlet system. Near TDC, the spark plug ignites the compressed charge.

STROKE 02 · DOWNWARD

Power + Exhaust + Transfer

බලය, පිටාරය සහ මිශ්‍රණ මාරුව

Combustion pushes the piston down and compresses the fresh charge in the crankcase. The exhaust port opens first, then the transfer port. Fresh charge enters the cylinder and helps push out burnt gases (scavenging).

Important: Two-stroke designs vary. This explanation describes a common small crankcase-scavenged petrol engine. Large two-stroke diesel engines often use separate air-scavenging equipment and fuel injection; do not apply this petrol-engine diagram to them.
